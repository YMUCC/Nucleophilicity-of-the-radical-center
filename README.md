<p align="justify">
  Radical chemistry plays a pivotal role in synthesis and chemical biology, yet predicting radical reactivity remains challenging due to transient intermediates and complex electronic effects. Herein, we develop an interpretable machine learning framework for predicting atom-centered nucleophilicity of radicals. A dataset of nucleophilicity for 633 radicals was constructed using DFT-derived NPA charges, and 11 physically meaningful descriptors were selected to train ten ML algorithms. The Random Forest model exhibited better performance, achieving an R² of 0.858 and MAE of 0.053. External validation on 26 additional radicals, including metal-containing species, confirmed its reliable prediction. Descriptor analysis using feature importance and PDP revealed the key roles of electronegativity, steric effects, and neighboring atom interactions in regulating nucleophilicity at radical center. This framework provides an efficient and interpretable strategy for prediction of radical nucleophilicity and mechanistic understanding.
